# Pop Charts: A No-Subsidy Use Of LMSR For Prediction Market Bootstrapping

Matthew Brown

August 2026

WORKING DRAFT, rev. 0.6

## Abstract

A new prediction market needs a credible price before it has liquidity, but in a thin market every trade moves the price violently. Subsidized market makers solve this for a handful of flagship markets and fail for the long tail. This paper describes a way to use the Logarithmic Market Scoring Rule (LMSR) that needs no subsidy. Before graduation the market runs a **virtual** LMSR: the liquidity parameter `b` shapes how smoothly the price moves but is backed by no bankroll, and every bet is a **priced intent** — a receipt recording the exact segment of the price curve it bought — rather than a final outcome token.

Graduation converts intents into a standard fixed-payout market in the style of Polymarket's Conditional Token Framework (CTF). A band-pass clearing rule retains exactly the price bands that both YES and NO demand crossed in opposite directions, mints fully collateralized complete sets there, and refunds every other segment at the exact path cost the bettor paid. We prove that the collateral locked at graduation equals the maximum settlement obligation exactly — zero deficit, zero surplus, no external capital.

Capital is committed only where it has actually met a counterparty. A band of a bettor's range locks the moment any opposite-side receipt covers it, and every band nobody ever opposed may be withdrawn before clearing. We prove this withdrawal rule leaves the matched market cap unchanged, so no participant can withdraw their way into or out of a graduation, and the classical pump-and-withdraw attack is foreclosed rather than merely priced.

The remaining tradeoffs are explicit: opposed capital is locked until the market clears or expires, fills are partial and never guaranteed, and a bettor's worst case is a full refund. In exchange, a market opens at any prior, discovers prices from its first trade, and hands fully backed exposure to ordinary infrastructure the moment real opposing demand exists.

## 1. The Bootstrapping Problem

A new market needs a price before it has liquidity, but without liquidity it cannot discover a price worth trusting. When the book is thin, a small order swings the displayed odds, so the price reflects order size more than belief — and the traders who could correct it stay away precisely because it is noisy.

The direct fix is subsidy: seed each market with enough market-maker capital that early trades barely move the price. This works for a few flagship markets and scales poorly past them. A protocol launching many markets must hold a large treasury, accept shallow prices, or burn capital on markets that never attract demand — and it may simply not have the money. A central limit order book has the opposite problem: it matches precisely once makers and takers arrive, but an empty book discovers nothing.

LMSR is the closest primitive to what launch wants. It quotes from its first block, moves smoothly under sparse flow, and expresses probabilities directly rather than through reserve ratios. The catch is funding: in conventional LMSR the parameter `b` is tied to a bounded market-maker loss that someone must bankroll, so every new market needs a subsidy again.

This paper keeps LMSR's price-discovery surface and removes the funding requirement by changing what a pre-graduation trade **is**. The parameter `b` becomes virtual smoothness, not funded inventory; bets become priced intents, not final purchases; and graduation becomes the explicit step that decides which intents become claims.

```text
Stage 1 — Bootstrap: a virtual LMSR prices intents. No subsidy, no final fills.
Stage 2 — Graduation: band-pass clearing converts overlapping intents into complete sets.
Stage 3 — Standard market: CTF-style YES/NO tokens trade on an ordinary venue.
```

The third stage is the point. The virtual curve is scaffolding — it discovers a price and gathers committed demand for a market that does not yet deserve a funded book — and it is built to retire itself the moment the market does.

## 2. Background And The Funding Gap

The destination is a fully collateralized fixed-payout market. A binary outcome token pays a fixed amount, and a complete set is worth exactly its collateral:

```math
\begin{aligned}
1\,\mathrm{YES} &\mapsto 1 && \text{if YES resolves true} \\
1\,\mathrm{NO} &\mapsto 1 && \text{if NO resolves true} \\
1\,\mathrm{YES}+1\,\mathrm{NO} &= 1 && \text{unit of collateral}
\end{aligned}
```

This constraint is what separates prediction-market launch from token launch. A bonding curve can sell a token with no fixed redemption value; a prediction market cannot safely sell 100 YES claims at 0.05, collect 5 units, and later owe 100 if YES wins — unless something funds the missing 95. Polymarket's [Conditional Token Framework (CTF)](https://docs.polymarket.com/trading/ctf/overview) is the reference destination: YES and NO are ordinary tokens backed by complete sets, tradable on standard venues. The problem is not that this destination is wrong; it is that a brand-new market must discover a price and attract opposing demand before any complete sets exist.

LMSR prices trades from the cost function, with marginal prices that are complementary probabilities:

```math
C(q_{\mathrm{yes}},q_{\mathrm{no}})=b\ln\!\left(e^{q_{\mathrm{yes}}/b}+e^{q_{\mathrm{no}}/b}\right),
\qquad
P_{\mathrm{yes}}=\frac{e^{q_{\mathrm{yes}}/b}}{e^{q_{\mathrm{yes}}/b}+e^{q_{\mathrm{no}}/b}},
\qquad
P_{\mathrm{no}}=1-P_{\mathrm{yes}}
```

Its state is a probability score, not reserve inventory, so it opens at any prior and moves smoothly under sparse flow. But a binary LMSR that sells final tokens can lose up to

```math
\mathrm{max\ loss}=b\ln 2
```

and someone must bankroll that bound. That single requirement is the funding gap this paper closes. The alternatives close it only by giving something up:

| Mechanism | Strength at launch | What it gives up |
|---|---|---|
| Subsidized LMSR | Instant smooth quotes and guaranteed fills | Needs a funded loss budget per market |
| Pari-mutuel pool | Solvent with zero subsidy | No fixed payout at entry odds; never becomes CTF-style |
| Reserve AMM | Familiar passive liquidity | Real reserves take outcome risk; virtual reserves cannot pay winners |
| Central limit order book | Precise matching at scale | An empty book discovers nothing |
| Virtual LMSR + band-pass clearing | Immediate discovery, no subsidy, solvent graduation | Fills deferred, partial, not guaranteed; capital locked until clearing |

The last row is this paper. The rest makes its two halves precise: how a virtual LMSR prices intents, and how clearing turns the compatible ones into a solvent market.

## 3. Virtual LMSR

### What The State Means

Before graduation the market runs full LMSR math, but the meaning of the state changes:

```text
q_yes, q_no are demand-pricing state, not sold inventory.
b is virtual smoothness, not a funded loss budget.
every bet is a priced intent; its collateral stays escrowed.
no final YES/NO token exists before graduation clearing.
```

Because no final token is sold, no loss bound needs funding. The curve can be made arbitrarily smooth — `b` is a free per-market design parameter — without anyone underwriting `b ln 2`.

### Starting Away From Even Odds

Markets often should not open at 50/50. Work in the path coordinate `r` and its price:

```math
r=q_{\mathrm{yes}}-q_{\mathrm{no}},
\qquad
P_{\mathrm{yes}}(r)=\frac{1}{1+e^{-r/b}},
\qquad
P_{\mathrm{no}}(r)=\frac{1}{1+e^{r/b}}
```

To open at probability `P0`, start the state at:

```math
r_0=b\ln\!\left(\frac{P_0}{1-P_0}\right)
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
r_0\big|_{P_0=0.05}=b\ln\!\left(\frac{0.05}{0.95}\right)\approx -2.9444\,b
```

The offset is a coordinate, not collateral. The market can quote 5% without anyone holding a deep reserve of cheap YES claims — something a reserve-based AMM cannot do without either real imbalanced inventory or unbacked virtual depth.

### What The Quote Promises

The displayed price is an implied probability and the exact rule applied to the next intent. It is not a fill: quoting 35% does not mean anyone can settle exposure at 35%, because no opposing collateral has been proven to exist there. A virtual LMSR can quote any price without evidence of opposite-side demand. Treating its buys as final tokens would silently reintroduce the missing subsidy as bad debt. Instead the mechanism records, for every bet, exactly which part of the curve it bought, and defers to graduation the only question that matters for solvency: did YES and NO demand cross the same bands in opposite directions?

## 4. Receipts: Priced Intents Over A Range

### The Record

Each pre-graduation buy creates a receipt — the durable record of one priced intent:

```math
\begin{aligned}
\sigma_\ell &\in \{\mathrm{YES},\mathrm{NO}\} && \text{side} \\
s_\ell &: \text{provisional shares}, & c_\ell &: \text{escrowed cost basis} \\
[a_\ell,\,z_\ell] &: \text{path interval on } r, & \tau_\ell &: \text{sequence index}
\end{aligned}
```

A YES buy of `s` shares raises `q_yes` by `s`, hence raises `r` by `s`; a NO buy lowers `r` by `s`. Every receipt therefore sweeps an interval whose width is its share count, and pays the marginal price of its side integrated over that interval:

```math
s_\ell=z_\ell-a_\ell,
\qquad
c_\ell=\int_{a_\ell}^{z_\ell}P_{\sigma_\ell}(r)\,dr
```

Both integrals have closed forms. Over a price band whose lower and upper YES probabilities are `P_lo` and `P_hi`, the cost to sweep it on each side is positive and equals:

```math
\mathrm{YES\_cost}=\int P_{\mathrm{yes}}=b\ln\!\left(\frac{1-P_{\mathrm{lo}}}{1-P_{\mathrm{hi}}}\right),
\qquad
\mathrm{NO\_cost}=\int P_{\mathrm{no}}=b\ln\!\left(\frac{P_{\mathrm{hi}}}{P_{\mathrm{lo}}}\right)
```

A YES buy crosses the band upward and a NO buy crosses it downward, but both pay the same per-side integral over the same band. Their sum is the band width — the seed of the solvency proof in Section 6. Every number in this paper's worked example comes from these two formulas. A receipt's average price `c_ℓ / s_ℓ` is fine for display but plays no role in clearing: cost is distributed unevenly along the interval, because the marginal price changes as the trade moves the curve.

### Buying A Range

It is worth saying plainly what a pre-graduation buyer is buying, because it is not a token at one price. A receipt is a priced intent over a **range** of odds. A trader who moves YES from 20% to 40% has bought a continuous strip of provisional shares — one infinitesimal slice at every price between 20% and 40% — paying each slice exactly the marginal price at which it moved the curve. The receipt behaves like a dense ladder of tiny limit orders, one resting at every price in the range, none of them yet filled.

Graduation later decides, slice by slice, which parts of the ladder became real. Every slice ends in exactly one of two states: converted into a fixed-payout share at the price recorded for that slice, or refunded at the price recorded for that slice. Nothing settles at an average that mixes slices, and nothing is kept without the collateral to pay it.

A trader who enters by budget `c` rather than share count gets the shares by inverting the cost function. With `A = e^{q_yes/b}` and `B = e^{q_no/b}`:

```math
s_{\mathrm{yes}}=b\ln\!\left(\frac{e^{c/b}(A+B)-B}{A}\right),
\qquad
s_{\mathrm{no}}=b\ln\!\left(\frac{e^{c/b}(A+B)-A}{B}\right)
```

### Rights, And The Withdrawal Rule

A receipt grants exactly three rights: to participate in clearing, to receive fixed-payout tokens for every matched segment at that segment's recorded cost, and to a refund of every unmatched segment at its recorded cost. It does **not** grant a guaranteed fill, a final position before clearing, any resolution payout before graduation, or the ability to transfer or sell the receipt.

It does grant one thing earlier revisions of this paper withheld: the right to take back the part of the range nobody ever took the other side of.

**The rule.** A band of a receipt is *opposed* once any live opposite-side receipt covers it. Opposed bands lock — for both receipts, permanently, until clearing. Every other band may be withdrawn at any time before the freeze, refunding that band's own recorded path cost, less the withdrawal fee of Section 7.

The rule works because opposition is monotone where retention is not. A receipt's *retained* share of a band is `φ_{σ,k} = m_k / (count on its own side)`, which falls whenever a same-side receipt arrives and rises whenever an opposite-side one does. It is a running estimate of an election that has not been held, and no part of it can be vested early. Whether a band has *ever been opposed*, by contrast, only accumulates: nothing un-opposes a band, because withdrawal can only ever remove unopposed capital.

The rule is deliberately conservative. An unopposed band has `m_k = 0` and is therefore guaranteed to refund in full at clearing, so withdrawal returns only capital that was already certain to come back — never a segment whose fate is still open. A bettor's unopposed range is strictly smaller than their eventual refund, because bands where their side is crowded will also partly refund, and those stay locked. Lemma 3 draws the consequence: such a withdrawal cannot move `F`, so no holder can withdraw their way into or out of a graduation.

This also forecloses the manipulation earlier revisions feared. *Pump the odds, attract the other side, withdraw* fails at the second step: the moment anyone takes the other side, the overlap locks and the pump is committed. What remains possible is to move the display and retract it while nobody responds — in which case nobody was mispriced, because nobody traded. Section 7's withdrawal fee is what prices that residue.

Two costs are real. First, a receipt is no longer one interval: withdrawing a band from the middle of a range splits it, so a receipt is in general a finite union of intervals, and both the sweep and the storage layer must carry that. Second, a holder can be locked by someone choosing to oppose them — but at a price the mechanism fixes rather than leaves open, since YES and NO costs over a band sum to its width, so opposing a receipt costs the complement of what its holder paid. The opposing party then holds a real receipt that clears against them or refunds. Locking a receipt is indistinguishable from taking its other side, because it *is* taking its other side; what the holder loses is not their capital but the option to change their mind.

Capital that has not met a counterparty is therefore liquid, and capital that has is committed until clearing. Section 8 quantifies what the committed part can become.

## 5. Band-Pass Graduation Clearing

### Why Aggregate Matching Is Not Enough

The natural shortcut — match total YES against total NO and prorate the heavier side — is unsafe, for two independent reasons. First, equal totals do not imply the sides met: a YES wave from 5% to 20% and a NO wave from 80% to 65% can have identical share counts while sharing no price band, so retaining both would mint claims from demand that never opposed itself. Second, a receipt's average price is lossless only if the whole receipt is retained or refunded. A YES receipt that moved 50% to 60% paid cheap slices near 50% and expensive ones near 60%; if only its 55%–60% top finds opposing demand, the retained exposure costs what **that** segment cost, not the receipt's average. Settling partial fills at an average over- and under-collateralizes band by band. Safe clearing must therefore compare the actual paths, at full resolution.

### The Sweep

Clearing runs over a frozen receipt book. Each receipt is already a set of intervals on `r` — one for a receipt never withdrawn from, several for one that has been — so the algorithm is a deterministic sweep:

1. Collect every interval endpoint, sort, and deduplicate.
2. Sweep each adjacent band `[r_k, r_{k+1}]`, skipping zero-width and dust bands.
3. Count the YES receipts whose interval covers the whole band (`Y_k`) and the NO receipts that do (`N_k`).
4. If either count is zero, retain nothing in the band.
5. Otherwise retain the scarce side fully and the crowded side pro-rata, with `m_k = min(Y_k, N_k)`.
6. Credit each covering receipt with its retained band shares and band cost.
7. After the sweep, refund every receipt's unretained path cost.

Because the endpoint set contains every receipt boundary, each band lies fully inside or fully outside any given receipt — there are no partial-coverage cases, and a receipt split by withdrawal is handled by the same statement, since the gap it leaves is simply a band it no longer covers. The sweep is indifferent to time: a receipt matches any opposite-side receipt that covered the same band, whether it traded earlier or later. (Sequence indices exist for deterministic rounding tie-breaks, not priority; FIFO matching would be a coherent alternative policy layered on the same solvency math.)

### Band Arithmetic

For band `k` with width `w_k = r_{k+1} − r_k`, define the per-side retained fractions and the matched count:

```math
\varphi_{\mathrm{Y},k}=\frac{m_k}{Y_k},
\qquad
\varphi_{\mathrm{N},k}=\frac{m_k}{N_k},
\qquad
m_k=\min(Y_k,N_k)
```

Each YES receipt covering the band keeps fraction `φ_{Y,k}` of it, and symmetrically for NO. So a receipt's totals are sums over the bands it covers:

```math
\mathrm{retained\_shares}_\ell=\!\!\sum_{k:\,B_k\subseteq I_\ell}\!\!\varphi_{\sigma_\ell,k}\,w_k,
\qquad
\mathrm{retained\_cost}_\ell=\!\!\sum_{k:\,B_k\subseteq I_\ell}\!\!\varphi_{\sigma_\ell,k}\!\int_{B_k}\!P_{\sigma_\ell}(r)\,dr,
\qquad
\mathrm{refund}_\ell=c_\ell-\mathrm{retained\_cost}_\ell
```

Proration scales a receipt's band shares and band cost by the same fraction, so it changes how much of a band a receipt keeps — never the per-share price of what it keeps. The market cap contributed by the band is `F_k = m_k w_k`. The next section proves this is exactly collateralized.

### Threshold And Handoff

Graduation is decided by the one quantity clearing actually proves — the path-compatible filled market cap `F = Σ_k m_k w_k` — not by provisional volume, open interest, total escrow, or the displayed price, all of which can be large while real overlap is near zero. The market graduates iff `F ≥ graduation_threshold`. If the close time arrives first, the no-subsidy rule is unconditional: every receipt is refunded in full (`retained_shares = 0`, `refund = c`). Sub-threshold overlap may be solvent, but the threshold defines the minimum viable market and keeps graduation from minting fragile dust markets; it can be paired with non-price gates (unique participants, concentration limits) that sit outside the clearing math.

Graduation outputs exactly what a standard venue needs: retained YES and NO balances, locked collateral of one unit per complete set, and the market's metadata and resolution rules. That exposure trades on any venue preserving the complete-set invariant — a CLOB, a CTF-compatible AMM, or a hybrid — which inherits fully backed tokens and is never asked to rescue a receipt. This is the plot resolving: the virtual curve exists to reach this handoff, and retires once it does, because a CTF-style market is simply better once it has liquidity.

## 6. Solvency At Graduation

This section proves the central claim: after clearing, the collateral locked behind retained claims equals their maximum settlement obligation exactly, using only the escrow bettors already paid.

### Model

Fix `b > 0` and the frozen book of receipts. The marginal prices are complementary probabilities:

```math
P_{\mathrm{yes}}(r)=\frac{1}{1+e^{-r/b}},
\qquad
P_{\mathrm{no}}(r)=\frac{1}{1+e^{r/b}},
\qquad
P_{\mathrm{yes}}(r)+P_{\mathrm{no}}(r)=1 .
```

A purchase of an infinitesimal `dr` of shares on side `σ` at state `r` advances `r` by `dr` (YES) or retreats it by `dr` (NO) and costs `P_σ(r)\,dr`. Receipt `ℓ` thus has side `σ_ℓ`, live support `I_ℓ ⊂ ℝ`, width `s_ℓ = |I_ℓ|`, and escrowed cost `c_ℓ = ∫_{I_ℓ} P_{σ_ℓ}`. A receipt begins life as a single interval `[a_ℓ, z_ℓ]`; withdrawal under Section 4 removes sub-bands, so in general `I_ℓ` is a finite union of disjoint intervals. Nothing below depends on which: the endpoints of all intervals partition the occupied axis into bands `B_k = [r_k, r_{k+1}]` of width `w_k`, and every `I_ℓ` is a union of bands. Let `𝒴_k`, `𝒩_k` be the YES and NO receipts whose support covers `B_k`, with counts `Y_k`, `N_k` and `m_k = min(Y_k, N_k)`. The clearing rule of Section 5 assigns each covering receipt the band fraction `φ_{σ,k}` defined there.

Define the aggregates:

```math
F=\sum_k m_k w_k,
\qquad
L=\sum_\ell \mathrm{retained\_cost}_\ell,
\qquad
E=\sum_\ell c_\ell,
\qquad
R=\sum_\ell \mathrm{refund}_\ell .
```

`F` is the retained market cap, `L` the locked collateral, `E` the total escrow, `R` the total refunds.

### Lemma 1 — Local Funding Identity

For every band `k`, the collateral retained inside it equals `m_k w_k`:

```math
\underbrace{\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{Y}_k}\varphi_{\mathrm{Y},k}\!\int_{B_k}\!P_{\mathrm{yes}}}_{\text{retained YES cost}}
+\underbrace{\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{N}_k}\varphi_{\mathrm{N},k}\!\int_{B_k}\!P_{\mathrm{no}}}_{\text{retained NO cost}}
=m_k\!\int_{B_k}\!\bigl(P_{\mathrm{yes}}+P_{\mathrm{no}}\bigr)
=m_k\!\int_{B_k}\!1
=m_k w_k .
```

**Proof.** Each of the `Y_k` YES receipts contributes `φ_{Y,k} = m_k/Y_k` of `∫_{B_k} P_yes`, so the YES sum is `m_k ∫_{B_k} P_yes`; likewise the NO sum is `m_k ∫_{B_k} P_no`. Add them and apply complementarity `P_yes + P_no = 1`. If `m_k = 0` both sides vanish. ∎

### Lemma 2 — Balanced Complete Sets

Band `k` retains equal YES and NO shares, both equal to `m_k w_k`:

```math
\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{Y}_k}\varphi_{\mathrm{Y},k}\,w_k
=Y_k\cdot\frac{m_k}{Y_k}\,w_k
=m_k w_k
=N_k\cdot\frac{m_k}{N_k}\,w_k
=\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{N}_k}\varphi_{\mathrm{N},k}\,w_k .
```

So the band yields `m_k w_k` YES shares and `m_k w_k` NO shares: exactly `m_k w_k` complete sets, each redeemable for one unit of collateral. ∎

### Lemma 3 — Withdrawal Invariance

Let receipt `ℓ` withdraw a sub-band `B_j ⊆ I_ℓ` that is unopposed, meaning no opposite-side receipt covers `B_j`. Then `F`, `L`, and every other receipt's retained shares and retained cost are unchanged.

**Proof.** Withdrawal replaces `I_ℓ` by `I_ℓ \ B_j` and returns `∫_{B_j} P_{σ_ℓ}` to its holder. The covering sets of every band other than `B_j` are untouched, so it suffices to examine `B_j`.

Write the side counts before withdrawal as `(Y_j, N_j)` and suppose without loss of generality that `σ_ℓ = YES`. "Unopposed" means `N_j = 0`, hence

```math
m_j=\min(Y_j,\,0)=0 .
```

After withdrawal the counts are `(Y_j − 1, 0)`, so `m_j' = min(Y_j − 1, 0) = 0` as well. The band therefore contributes `m_j w_j = 0` to `F` both before and after, and by Lemma 1 its retained collateral is `0` both before and after. Every other band keeps its counts, so every other receipt keeps its fractions `φ_{σ,k}`. Summing over bands leaves `F` and `L` unchanged. ∎

**Corollary — graduation is immune to withdrawal.** Since `F` is invariant under every withdrawal the rule permits, and graduation is decided by `F ≥ graduation_threshold`, no participant can withdraw their way into or out of a graduation. In particular the race between freezing a market and withdrawing from it has no payoff, and no holder — however large — holds a veto over the transition.

The lemma is also the exact reason the rule cannot be loosened to "withdraw whatever clearing would refund." That larger set includes bands where the holder's own side is crowded, which have `m_k ≥ 1`. Removing a YES receipt from such a band takes `Y_k` to `Y_k − 1`, and the effect splits: if `Y_k > N_k` strictly, `m_k = N_k` is unchanged and `F` holds, but the surviving holders' fraction `m_k / (Y_k − 1)` rises, so the withdrawal silently redistributes other people's fills. If `Y_k ≤ N_k` — the withdrawing side attains the minimum — then `m_k` itself falls and `F` falls with it. Only the unopposed set is inert in both respects.

### Theorem — Exact Collateralization

After clearing:

1. **Locked equals retained cap.** `L = F`.
2. **Exact backing.** The locked collateral equals the maximum settlement obligation. Under any binary resolution the winning side holds `F` shares owed one unit each, and `L = F` funds them with zero deficit and zero surplus.
3. **Conservation, no bad debt.** Every receipt satisfies `0 ≤ refund_ℓ ≤ c_ℓ` and `retained_cost_ℓ + refund_ℓ = c_ℓ`; summing, `E = L + R`. No external capital is used. Refunds paid early under the withdrawal rule are part of `R`, not an exception to it: Lemma 3 leaves `L` untouched, and the withdrawn band's cost simply moves from the escrow term to the refund term at the moment it is taken rather than at the freeze.

**Proof. (1)** Summing Lemma 1 over all bands, `L = Σ_k m_k w_k = F`.

**(2)** By Lemma 2 each band holds equal YES and NO shares, so the whole retained book is complete sets totaling `F`. At resolution exactly one side pays; that side holds `Σ_k m_k w_k = F` shares, owed `F` units. By (1) the locked collateral is `L = F`. Hence obligation and backing coincide for either outcome — the most that can ever be owed is `F`, and exactly `F` is held.

**(3)** The bands covered by `I_ℓ` partition it, and `0 ≤ φ_{σ_ℓ,k} ≤ 1`, so

```math
0\le \mathrm{retained\_cost}_\ell
=\!\!\sum_{k:\,B_k\subseteq I_\ell}\!\!\varphi_{\sigma_\ell,k}\!\int_{B_k}\!P_{\sigma_\ell}
\;\le\;\sum_{k:\,B_k\subseteq I_\ell}\int_{B_k}\!P_{\sigma_\ell}
=\int_{I_\ell}\!P_{\sigma_\ell}=c_\ell .
```

Thus `refund_ℓ = c_ℓ − retained_cost_ℓ ∈ [0, c_ℓ]` and `retained_cost_ℓ + refund_ℓ = c_ℓ`. Summing over receipts, `L + R = E`. Every unit paid out is a unit some bettor escrowed; nothing is socialized or invented. ∎

### Tightness And The Necessity Of Overlap

The rule retains the most that can be backed without subsidy, and no more. Consider a one-sided band (`m_k = 0`, no opposing receipt). Retaining a lone YES share there at state `r` would lock only its payment `P_yes(r) < 1` against an obligation of `1`, a deficit of

```math
1-P_{\mathrm{yes}}(r)=P_{\mathrm{no}}(r)>0
```

that nothing in the book funds. This is exactly the missing `b ln 2` subsidy reappearing locally. Band-pass clearing refuses such shares — hence the refund of all non-overlapping segments — and that refusal is what the no-subsidy guarantee requires. Conversely, wherever `m_k ≥ 1`, the matched opposing payment supplies precisely the complementary `P_no(r)` (or `P_yes(r)`), closing the gap to one. Overlap is therefore both necessary and sufficient for a segment to be retainable, and the clearing rule retains every overlapping unit. The worked example in Section 9 verifies all three theorem claims numerically.

## 7. Fees And Post-Graduation Liquidity

The mechanism as described so far charges nothing, and the proof of Section 6 depends on that: every unit paid out is a unit some bettor escrowed. A real deployment will charge something. The rule for doing so without disturbing the proof is unconditional — **fees appear as their own term, never inside the solvency identity.**

### Two Fees, Outside Escrow

- An **entry fee** `φ_in`, charged on a receipt's cost at purchase.
- A **withdrawal fee** `φ_out`, charged on the recorded cost of any band withdrawn under Section 4.

Neither touches escrow, and neither is earned at the moment it is collected. A buyer of a receipt costing `c` transfers `c(1 + φ_in)`, of which `c` becomes escrow and `c·φ_in` is held aside. A withdrawal of a band whose recorded cost is `c_j` returns `c_j(1 − φ_out)`.

The entry fee is charged on the whole receipt but **earned only on the part that matches**. At clearing the protocol keeps `φ_in · retained_cost_ℓ` from each receipt and returns the rest, so every refunded unit comes back carrying the fee prepaid on it. Because `L = F`, the aggregate is exact:

```math
\text{fees earned}
=\varphi_{\mathrm{in}}\sum_\ell \mathrm{retained\_cost}_\ell
=\varphi_{\mathrm{in}}L
=\varphi_{\mathrm{in}}F
```

Writing `E` for all collateral the market receives and `Φ` for what it ultimately keeps, the accounting becomes

```math
E = R + L + \Phi ,
\qquad\text{with}\qquad
E - \Phi = R + L
```

so the Theorem of Section 6 is untouched: it is a statement about escrow, and `Φ` was never escrow and never backs a claim. A fee that cannot be written this way — one taken out of `L`, or netted silently against refunds — would break exact collateralization, because `L = F` has no slack in it. There is no room in this mechanism for an implicit fee.

Collecting up front is forced rather than merely convenient. The fee cannot be billed at clearing instead: a fully filled receipt has no refund to deduct it from, and taking it from `L` is precisely what the identity forbids.

Two properties follow, and both are worth keeping deliberately. **The entry fee is a success fee** — a market that never reaches its threshold refunds it in full alongside escrow, so the protocol is paid only when it delivers a graduated market. And **the withdrawal rule keeps the promise Section 4 made**: an unopposed band never matched, so the fee prepaid on it was never earned and returns with it, and a withdrawing bettor pays exactly `φ_out` of what they take back. The withdrawal fee is the only unconditionally earned money in the mechanism.

### What The Withdrawal Fee Actually Buys

Not solvency: Lemma 3 already guarantees withdrawals cannot move `F`. Not protection from pump-and-withdraw: the lock rule forecloses it, because the pump commits the moment anyone responds. The fee prices exactly one residue — moving the displayed odds and retracting them while nobody responds.

That harm is external to the market. Nobody inside was mispriced, since nobody traded; the damage is a wrong number that someone may have acted on elsewhere. Nothing inside the mechanism calibrates the rate, and it is more honest to say so than to derive a false optimum. What can be stated is the shape. Walking a market from `P_0` to `P_1` on the YES side and then retracting costs

```math
\varphi_{\mathrm{out}}\cdot b\ln\!\left(\frac{1-P_0}{1-P_1}\right)
```

so the fee multiplies the curve's own resistance rather than acting as an independent lever. Choosing `b` remains the primary control over how expensive the display is to move; `φ_out` is a coefficient on it.

One asymmetry deserves stating plainly, because it argues for a low rate. A withdrawing bettor is collecting a refund the mechanism had already guaranteed them, early. A rate set high enough to deter a determined manipulator is paid overwhelmingly by honest impatience, since the manipulator faces the same rate and has already decided the display is worth buying.

### Seeding The Graduated Market

The natural destination for `Φ` is the venue the market graduates into, which begins life with fully backed tokens and no liquidity to trade them against. The fee pot can supply both sides without touching escrow.

Let `p*` be the price the market clears at, and let the venue hold one pool per outcome against collateral. Mint `m` complete sets from the pot — costing `m`, since a set is one unit — and split the remaining `Φ − m` as `c_Y` into the YES pool and `c_N` into the NO pool. Requiring each pool to be balanced at `p*`, meaning its token side and collateral side carry equal value, gives `c_Y = m·p*` and `c_N = m(1 − p*)`. Adding those and substituting the budget:

```math
c_Y+c_N=m\bigl(p^{*}+(1-p^{*})\bigr)=m,
\qquad
c_Y+c_N=\Phi-m
\;\Longrightarrow\;
m=\tfrac{\Phi}{2}
```

The split is therefore fixed by the balance requirement rather than chosen: mint `Φ/2` complete sets, deposit `Φ/2` YES with `(Φ/2)p*` collateral into the YES pool, and `Φ/2` NO with `(Φ/2)(1 − p*)` collateral into the NO pool. A pot of 100 graduating at 35% mints 50 sets and seeds 50 YES against 17.50, and 50 NO against 32.50. The minted sets are fully backed by their own deposit, so this adds collateralized supply beside the `F` sets clearing produced rather than diluting them.

**Seeded liquidity does not survive resolution, and must not be mistaken for a treasury asset.** A position in a market that settles to 0 or 1 is fully exposed to divergence: as the price converges, arbitrageurs take the appreciating side and leave the pool holding the other. For a full-range constant-product position seeded at 35%, the terminal value is roughly 88% of simply holding the same assets if YES wins, and roughly 5% if YES loses. The asymmetry is structural, not a parameter choice — the losing token goes to zero and the pool is holding most of it.

Seeding is therefore a service to traders during the market's life, and a deployment must either withdraw protocol liquidity before resolution or bound its range tightly enough to cap the loss. Leaving a full-range position to settle donates it to arbitrageurs.

**Fees alone cannot seed a usable pool, at any rate.** Depth per side is `Φ/2`, so as a fraction of matched cap it is exactly `φ_in/2` — 5% depth would demand a 10% entry fee, 10% depth a 20% one. At a 1% fee the pool holds half a percent of market cap, which a single holder trimming their position overwhelms. A deployment that wants the seed to function must top it up from its own capital; the fee pot is a contribution to that, not a substitute for it.

This is a genuine subsidy and the paper should not pretend otherwise — but it is a subsidy of *market making*, not of *solvency*. Complete sets remain backed one-for-one, the Theorem of Section 6 is untouched, and the bootstrap phase still costs the protocol nothing. What a deployment may now choose to fund is the liquidity of the venue the market graduates into, which is a different question from whether the claims are backed.

## 8. Fill Outcomes And Tradeoffs

Everything a pre-graduation bettor can experience follows from the clearing rule. Stating it from the bettor's side is what makes "you bought an intent over a range" a fair deal rather than a confusing one.

### Five Outcomes

A receipt with shares `s` and escrowed cost `c` ends in exactly one of four ways:

| Outcome | Condition | Retained shares | Refund |
|---|---|---|---|
| Withdrawn early | the bettor takes back bands nobody opposed | 0 for those bands | those bands' cost, less `φ_out` |
| No graduation | market never reaches the threshold | 0 | `c` (full) |
| Graduated, no overlap | no band of the receipt's range cleared | 0 | `c` (full) |
| Graduated, partial fill | some bands cleared, or the side was crowded | between 0 and `s` | `c` minus retained cost |
| Graduated, full fill | every band cleared and the side was scarce throughout | `s` | 0 |

The worst case is a full refund: the bettor recovers every escrowed unit, having paid only the time value of locking it. The best case is a full fill of `s` shares at the prices originally paid. Every case between is a per-band mixture of those two, never a blend at made-up prices. The first row is not a fifth fate so much as an early exercise of the second and third: it settles in advance exactly the bands whose refund was already certain, and by Lemma 3 it leaves every other participant's outcome untouched.

### Price Is Certain Even When Quantity Is Not

The retained share count moves with other traders — which bands the other side retraced, and how crowded the receipt's own side was in each:

```math
\mathrm{retained\_shares}_\ell=\sum_{k\in K}\frac{m_k}{Y_k}\,w_k,
\qquad
0\le\mathrm{retained\_shares}_\ell\le s_\ell .
```

What never moves is price. Each band a receipt keeps was priced by the receipt's own path integral at trade time; later traders decide which slices fill and in what fraction, but cannot make any filled slice more expensive. The effective price of a partial fill stays inside the range the bettor chose to sweep. For a YES receipt over `[u, v]` with any nonzero fill:

```math
P_{\mathrm{yes}}(u)\;\le\;
\frac{\mathrm{retained\_cost}_\ell}{\mathrm{retained\_shares}_\ell}
\;\le\;P_{\mathrm{yes}}(v) .
```

The worst-case fill price is the top of the bettor's own range; the best is the bottom. (For a NO receipt, the bound holds with `P_no` and the endpoints exchanged.) Both ends are reachable, since matching is time-symmetric.

### Payoff Bounds

Because the marginal price is strictly below one at every finite `r`, a nonzero fill has `retained_cost_ℓ < retained_shares_ℓ`. If the receipt's side wins, every retained share pays one unit:

```math
\mathrm{total\ received}_\ell
=\underbrace{(c_\ell-\mathrm{retained\_cost}_\ell)}_{\text{refund}}
+\underbrace{\mathrm{retained\_shares}_\ell}_{\text{payout}}
=c_\ell+\bigl(\mathrm{retained\_shares}_\ell-\mathrm{retained\_cost}_\ell\bigr)>c_\ell .
```

A winning bettor profits on any nonzero fill, by one minus the locked-in price per retained share. If the side loses:

```math
\mathrm{total\ received}_\ell=c_\ell-\mathrm{retained\_cost}_\ell,
\qquad
\mathrm{maximum\ loss}_\ell=\mathrm{retained\_cost}_\ell\le c_\ell .
```

A bettor can lose only the cost of shares that actually filled — never escrow that did not fill, and never more than was escrowed. There is no negative refund, no socialized loss, and no claim paid from anyone else's principal.

## 9. Worked Example

Let `b = 100` and let the market open at 20%, so `r_0 = 100 ln(0.20/0.80) ≈ −138.63`. Three traders act in sequence; every figure follows from the closed forms of Section 4 (for example, Alice's cost is `100 ln(0.80/0.60) = 100 ln(4/3) ≈ 28.77`):

Noah traded once yet matches both Alice (before him) and Bea (after him): coverage, not order, is what clears. Per receipt:

| Trader | Retained shares | Retained cost | Refund | Effective price |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Alice | 32.77 YES | 11.71 | 17.06 | 0.3573 |
| Bea | 11.41 YES | 3.71 | 3.71 | 0.3247 |
| Noah | 44.18 NO | 28.77 | 0 | 0.6511 |

Each row shows one behavior from Section 7. Alice is partially filled: her cheap 20%–30% slices refund, raising her effective price from 0.2933 to 0.3573 — inside her range bound `[0.20, 0.40]`. Bea is prorated: she keeps half her shares at exactly her 0.3247 average, because proration scales shares and cost together. Noah is scarce everywhere he traveled and fills completely. The Theorem holds to four decimals:

```math
\begin{aligned}
E &= 28.7682+7.4108+28.7682 = 64.9472 \\
R &= 17.0585+3.7054+0 = 20.7639 \\
L &= 22.8259+21.3574 = 44.1833 = F \\
L+R &= 64.9472 = E
\end{aligned}
```

With a threshold of 40, `F = 44.18 ≥ 40` graduates: 44.18 YES and 44.18 NO tokens mint against 44.18 locked units, and 20.76 units refund. Settlement is then a closed pool — winners paid entirely from losers' retained cost, refunded segments sitting outside the bet:

| Resolution | Alice net | Bea net | Noah net | Sum |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| YES resolves true | +21.06 | +7.71 | −28.77 | 0 |
| NO resolves true | −11.71 | −3.71 | +15.42 | 0 |

Both rows sum to zero: the graduated market is exactly the locked pool. Alice's worst case is losing her 11.71 retained cost, not her 28.77 escrow — the unfilled majority was already refunded. Had the threshold been 50, the book would not graduate and all three would be refunded in full.

A one-sided drift behaves the same way at larger scale. A lone YES intent that pushes a market from 5% to 35% is informative, but information is not collateral: below the bands that later opposing flow retraces, the whole segment refunds at path cost. Backfilling it would require the one thing it lacks — someone real on the other side — so paint-the-curve manipulation converts, at graduation, into the manipulator's own refund.

## 10. Limitations And Open Questions

**Opposed capital is still locked.** The withdrawal rule frees only what nobody took the other side of. Once a band is opposed it is committed until graduation, cancellation, or the close-time refund, and in an active two-sided market that is most of the book. Withdrawal improves capital efficiency at the edges; it is not free exit, and interfaces should not present it as one.

**Receipts are no longer single intervals.** Withdrawing an interior band splits a receipt, so storage, the sweep, and every wire format must carry a finite union of intervals. The solvency proof is indifferent to this — it only ever uses the band partition — but the implementation is not, and the fragmentation bound is a real engineering parameter rather than a formality.

**Being opposed is a way to be pinned.** A holder can be locked by anyone willing to take the other side of their range. The price is fixed by the mechanism at the complement of what the holder paid, and the opposing party takes on a genuine position rather than burning capital, so this is trading rather than griefing. But it is cheap where the complement is cheap: freezing a receipt bought near the top of the book costs a small fraction of what that holder committed.

**The withdrawal fee is uncalibrated.** Section 7 prices display manipulation, whose harm is external to the market. No quantity inside the mechanism determines the rate, so it is a judgement subject to revision against observed behavior rather than a derived optimum.

**No guaranteed fill.** A receipt may fill fully, partially, or not at all, depending on other traders' paths. The promise is exactness, not certainty: every slice settles at its own recorded price within the bounds of Section 8.

**Refund disappointment.** A popular one-sided market can end in a large refund event. This is the mechanism working — unbacked exposure must not survive graduation — but users who mistake provisional receipts for fills will read it as failure. Interfaces must label receipts as provisional and surface estimated retention honestly.

**Range cognition.** Buying an intent over a range is a genuinely different object from buying at a spot price, and no interface fully hides that. The ladder framing of Section 4 and the outcome table of Section 8 belong in the product, not just the paper.

**Graduation gaming.** The clearing math is indifferent to who trades, so an attacker may try to manufacture just enough overlap to graduate on favorable terms. Thresholds should be paired with non-price gates — unique-participant minimums, concentration limits, time-in-book — before a minimally matched book graduates.

Open parameters remain empirical and do not affect the solvency proof: how `b` should vary with category, volatility, and opening prior; what threshold yields viable post-graduation liquidity without refunding too many useful small markets; what rounding policy preserves deterministic clearing under integer arithmetic; what `φ_in` and `φ_out` should be, and whether `φ_out` is better charged on withdrawn cost or on withdrawn width, since width is what moves the display; how tightly seeded liquidity should be bounded and when it should be withdrawn ahead of resolution; and whether receipts can be made transferable without reopening manipulation and refund-ownership problems.

Transferability is now the same question as withdrawal wearing different clothes, and Lemma 3 suggests its answer has the same shape: a transfer moves ownership without changing any band's covering counts, so it leaves `F` invariant by inspection. What it does not settle is refund ownership across a partially withdrawn, fragmented receipt, which is where the difficulty actually lives.

## 11. Conclusion

A virtual LMSR is a strong bootstrapping surface: it quotes smoothly from the first trade, opens at any prior including deep longshots, and costs the protocol nothing to *back*, because what it sells are priced intents rather than claims it cannot honour. (A deployment may still choose to spend capital making a market in the graduated venue, as Section 7 describes; that is a commercial decision about liquidity, not a requirement of solvency.) Band-pass graduation clearing is what makes the free curve safe to build on — it matches only demand that actually opposed itself, locks one unit of collateral per retained complete set, and refunds every other segment at its recorded cost. Section 6 proves the result is exact: locked collateral equals the maximum settlement obligation, funded entirely by escrow bettors already paid.

The withdrawal rule of Section 4 sharpens that bargain rather than weakening it. Committing capital and committing to a *price* turn out to be separable: a bettor is bound exactly where someone met them, and free everywhere else. Because the released bands are precisely the ones clearing had already condemned to refund, `F` does not move, and the graduation decision cannot be bought, sold, or vetoed by anyone's exit.

The bargain is stated and bounded — opposed capital locked until clearing, fills partial and uncertain, prices exact, worst case a full refund — and the destination is always the standard market. Graduation hands fully collateralized CTF-style tokens to ordinary infrastructure and retires the scaffolding that produced them. The mechanism keeps separate three things launch designs usually conflate: probability discovery, fill, and settlement backing. A market is born without a bankroll, priced from its first block, and graduated only once real opposing demand has paid for the exposure it keeps.
